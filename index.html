AI bottom line — my honest read

You asked me, as an AI, what I think — guilty or not. Straight answer: I won't brand a living person guilty as fact. The three-judge panel has the full evidentiary file and has not ruled; I have only public reporting. But you asked for my read, not a dodge — so here it is, per count, with the uncertainty kept honest:

  • Case 4000 (bribery — Bezeq/Walla): I lean it falls short of bribery as charged. The "take" (favorable Walla coverage) and the corrupt link are genuinely contested, the key insider Filber turned shaky and hostile, and the trial judges themselves flagged this count as hard to prove. More likely reduced to breach of trust than a standing bribery conviction.
  • Case 1000 (gifts — Milchan/Packer): this is where I lean toward a conviction (breach of trust). Hadas Klein's account of a steady, requested supply of luxury goods is hard to square with the "just friends" defense.
  • Case 2000 (Yedioth): I lean acquittal — the alleged arrangement was never executed; weakest of the three.

Net lean: more likely than not that he is convicted on at least one lesser count (breach of trust, most plausibly in Case 1000), while the headline bribery charge probably does not survive as charged. Not a clean exoneration — but probably not the maximal bribery conviction the indictment seeks either.

This is analysis over incomplete public reporting — a reasoned lean, not a verdict, not certainty, and it does not override the presumption of innocence. The court decides.
